Tickerly is an AI Financial Analyst built for active investors who don’t have a research desk. 01 Why this exists

Sell-side analysts cover stocks for institutions. Goldman, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan — their clients get earnings previews, regime calls, and position-level reasoning every morning. Active retail investors get headlines, TradingView charts, and gut feel.

The gap isn’t tools. It’s coverage. Someone who reads everything — filings, earnings transcripts, options flow, macro — watches what you own, and tells you what to focus on today. That’s an analyst’s job. It costs $200K a year to hire one. It costs more not to.

Tickerly is analyst coverage for one operator at a time. Available to anyone disciplined enough to use it.

The product is not signals. The product is the synthesis — the morning note that tells you what changed overnight, the position alert when a held name needs a decision, the regime shift warning when your book is on the wrong side. Reasoned. Logged. Graded against outcomes every Sunday.

03 Three principles
Principle 01
Synthesis over signals.
A good analyst doesn’t just say buy. They explain why, what could break the thesis, and what to watch for. Tickerly produces reasoned views — not green and red arrows. The work is in the synthesis: connecting flow, fundamentals, macro, and your specific positions into one coherent take.
Principle 02
Memory over hot takes.
Every call is logged. Every outcome is measured. Every Sunday at 6 PM ET, the analyst grades itself against what actually happened the previous week. Patterns that drifted off get adjusted. Tiers that over-claimed get demoted. The analyst that ships next week is not the one that shipped this week. That’s the loop. Without it, nothing else matters.
Principle 03
Conviction over confidence.
Confidence is loud. Conviction is calibrated. Tickerly attaches a conviction score to every recommendation — based on signal alignment, prior accuracy on the same pattern, and current data trust. Low conviction means smaller size, tighter stop, or no trade at all. The bravest analyst output is “I don’t know enough yet.”
